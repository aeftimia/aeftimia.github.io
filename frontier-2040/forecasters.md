# Frontier 2040 — forecasters and recap

As of October 19, 2026. This complements [the Musk source ledger](/frontier-2040/). It records *claims and research assumptions*, **not** predictions we have validated as likely to occur. A person's prediction being authentic is separate from its correctness.

## Working premise for fiction (user-selected)

- Worldbuilding reference year: end of **2026**; narrative horizon **2040**.
- For Musk only, **assume for initial scenario construction that his claims eventually become true unless obviously implausible**, and make deadlines **twice as far from end-2026**: `adjusted_year = 2026 + 2 × (original_year - 2026)`.
- This is a **fiction-planning convention**, not a statistical demonstration that Musk will be right. Trackers' observed delay averages may exclude unfulfilled or censored forecasts.
- **Do not apply Musk's multiplier to other forecasters** without their own evidence.
- Attribute and date original statements, distinguish hard deadlines from aspirational scenarios, and separate capability demos from widespread adoption.

## Sam Altman — separate, unadjusted predictions

Source: [Three Observations](https://blog.samaltman.com/three-observations) (2025). Altman said the price of a given AI capability appeared to be falling by about **10× every 12 months** (descriptive trend, not guaranteed perpetual forecast); described AI agents becoming virtual co-workers; conjectured that *in a decade* people may achieve more than today's most impactful individuals (~2035). 

Source: [The Gentle Singularity](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity) (June 2025). Forecasts: **2026** systems that discover novel insights; **2027** robots able to do real-world tasks. No reliability multiplier assigned. Distinguish robots capable of *some* tasks from economical mass deployment.

## Dario Amodei — separate, unadjusted predictions

Source: [Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace) (October 2024). He said powerful AI **could** arrive as early as **2026**, while explicitly allowing much longer timelines. He then explored a *conditional* **5–10-year** period of accelerated science and medicine *after* powerful AI arrives. That scenario is not a firm prediction that all outlined medical advances occur by 2031–36.

## Firsthand Neuralink experiential material

Today's implanted interfaces primarily decode selected *intended actions*, not arbitrary private thoughts. Noland Arbaugh described progressively natural cursor control and electrode-thread displacement/recovery through software: [WIRED interview](https://www.wired.com/story/neuralink-first-patient-interview-noland-arbaugh-elon-musk/). Company reports feature robotic arm manipulation (Nick Wray), digital creation (Audrey Crews and Alex), and imagined-finger typing: [Neuralink participant update](https://neuralink.com/updates/two-years-of-telepathy/). Brad Smith, who has ALS, has used BCI-mediated computer interaction with generative AI assistance: [personal site](https://www.alscyborg.com/what-i-am-doing). Firsthand selected testimonials are useful for *subjective experience* but aren't population-level performance evidence. Neural-to-AI semantic exchange and meaningful high-bandwidth neural write-in remain unproven.

## Key epistemic distinctions

1. A source demonstrates **someone made a claim**; it doesn't demonstrate the claim will be realized.
2. An AI completing a benchmark ≠ replacing a profession; a robot demonstration ≠ billion-scale manufacture.
3. Neural cursor control ≠ reading concepts; stimulation ≠ implanting knowledge.
4. Original speaker's deadline, your adjusted fiction date, and observed real-world status must occupy different fields.
5. Developments in 2040 not directly predicted are **creative extrapolations**, not sourced facts.
